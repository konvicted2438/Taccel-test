# ------------- Important Notes -------------

1. It seems soft meshes MUST be a single volumetric entity i.e. use your GMSH converter function to
   turn a "soft_mesh.msh" file to "soft_mesh_vol.msh". The test is if PyVista can read in your mesh.
   It remains to be seen how this all works with .STL files.

2. Understand kinematic constraints applied in peg.py: 
   https://github.com/Taccel-Simulator/Taccel/blob/main/examples/peg.py

   This is how you'll learn to control your soft fingers in a bang-bang manner.

   It appears you need to apply soft kinematic constraints for affine/soft bodies AS WELL AS kinematic
   targets. The targets will update the position each timestep, and the constraints will help keep your
   system at the desired location. So add soft constraints at the top and bottom nodes of your gripper.
   And kinematic targets similar to those used in your WARP-PNCG-IPC simulation (i.e. sliding the top
   nodes down, then both sliding up for grasping). Confirm this tomorrow by commenting out kinematic constraints
   in the peg example.

   C.F.: In WARP-PNCG-IPC-CSIRO, integrator.is_fixed was a way you could fix certain nodes in a soft body. Under the
   hood, this zeroed all forces on these nodes at each timestep, effectively making these nodes massless. You could
   try to make your nodes massless too/or zero the forces at each timestep IF using soft kinematic constraints combined
   with kinematic targets doesn't produce your desired actuation/kinematic bang-bang control.

3. It appears that you can calculate total force in the z-direction via summing F_z due to contact and friction (i.e. 
   neglecting collision forces seems okay). I need to find out exactly what collision forces are for/what they do.
   TLDR: Calculate net_Fz as the sum of contact and friction forces, and ignore collision forces. This gave you 
   reasonable plots in "force_comparison_net_nocollisions.png" for drop_testsphere.py and soft_gripper_SDTO.py. In 
   both cases, including collision forces in net_Fz yielded unreasonable results i.e. "force_comparison_net3.png".
